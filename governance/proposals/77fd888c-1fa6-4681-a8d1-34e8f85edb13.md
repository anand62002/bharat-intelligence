## Abstract

arXiv:2604.22779v1 Announce Type: new 
Abstract: Enabling large language models (LLMs) to appropriately abstain from answering questions beyond their knowledge is crucial for mitigating hallucinations. While existing reinforcement learning methods foster autonomous abstention, they often compromise answer accuracy because their static reward mechanisms, agnostic to models' knowledge boundaries, drive models toward excessive caution. In this work, we propose KARL, a novel framework that continuously aligns an LLM's abstention behavior with its evolving knowledge boundary. KARL introduces two core innovations: a Knowledge-Boundary-Aware Reward that performs online knowledge boundary estimation using within-group response statistics, dynamically rewarding correct answers or guided abstention;

## Proposed Change

Implement KARL's Knowledge-Boundary-Aware Reward in governance/hallucination_detector.py by adding a dynamic confidence scoring module. For each agent's output, compute within-group response statistics (variance of scores across similar stocks in the same sector over rolling 7-day window) to estimate knowledge boundary. Modify the synthesis layer to flag signals where confidence falls below dynamically computed threshold (mean - 1.5*std) as 'ABSTAIN' rather than forcing BUY/HOLD/SELL, and track accuracy separately for high-confidence vs abstained predictions.

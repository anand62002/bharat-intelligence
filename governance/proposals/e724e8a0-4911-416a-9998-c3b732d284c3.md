## Abstract

While search agents demonstrate impressive capabilities in multi-step question answering, their robustness to poor-quality evidence remains under-explored. Such evidence is rare in benchmark corpora, and resulting failures are hard to reproduce. We propose DeepStress, a dynamic stress-testing framework that subjects agents to degraded evidence at controlled rates. It replaces the retrieval module with a synthetic environment where documents are generated dynamically for each query, under independent control of their trustworthiness, relevance, and factuality. ## Proposed Change

Add a `stress_test_evidence_quality()` function in `governance/hallucination_detector.py` that injects synthetic degraded documents (with controlled trustworthiness, relevance, and factuality flags) into each agent's data pipeline during weekly audits, then classifies agent outputs using a five-way outcome taxonomy (correct-confident, correct-cautious, incorrect-confident, incorrect-cautious, abstained) instead of binary pass/fail. Specifically, for `historical_rag` and `sentiment` agents—which are most vulnerable to poor-quality retrieved evidence—wrap their `retrieve()` and `fetch_rss_articles()` calls with a `EvidenceStressor` class that replaces N% of returned documents with LLM-generated misleading alternatives (via Claude Haiku, cheap) at configurable degradation rates (0%, 25%, 50%, 75%). Log results to a new `agent_stress_results` Supabase table and compute a Reliability-Adjusted Score (RAS) per agent, crediting HOLD/AVOID signals when evidence quality is flagged low rather than penalising caution.

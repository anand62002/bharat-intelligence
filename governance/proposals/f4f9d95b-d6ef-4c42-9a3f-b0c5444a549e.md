## Abstract

arXiv:2606.23989v1 Announce Type: new 
Abstract: End-to-end large language models (LLMs) produce fluent multi-document summaries but remain prone to hallucination, and the attributions they offer are typically coarse (whole documents or passages) and generated post hoc, leaving each summary statement hard to verify. We revisit the modular Extract--Select--Rewrite paradigm and recast its intermediate representation as the unit of attribution. We present CAMS, a Claim-Anchored Multi-document Summarization framework that (i) extracts atomic claims with token-level provenance from every source document, (ii) clusters equivalent claims across documents while flagging inter-source conflicts, (iii) selects a support-aware and salient subset, and (iv) rewrites the selection into a summary in which

## Proposed Change

Refactor agents/sentiment.py to replace the current RSS-to-summary flow with a CAMS-inspired claim extraction pipeline. Specifically, modify parse_news_article() to use Claude Haiku to extract atomic claims with span-level provenance (source article URL + sentence index), store claims in a new Supabase table 'news_claims' with columns (claim_text, source_url, source_span, extraction_date, conflict_flag). Update generate_sentiment_signal() to cluster equivalent claims across articles using pgvector cosine similarity on OpenAI embeddings, flag inter-source conflicts (e.g., contradictory earnings expectations), and return sentiment score based only on support-checked, non-conflicting claims—ensuring every sentiment justification in the detail dict traces back to verifiable source spans rather than entire articles.

## Abstract

When retrieved evidence contradicts an agent's prior beliefs, does it revise its answer, acknowledge uncertainty, or persist with an incorrect conclusion? Existing evaluations of agentic systems focus primarily on task success, offering limited insight into how agents handle such conflicts. We propose to evaluate agents on epistemic humility (EH): the agent's willingness to recognize, act on, and communicate uncertainty during task execution. We operationalize EH through three trajectory-level behavioral dimensions: Identify, Solve, and Escalate (ISE). ## Proposed Change

Add an 'uncertainty_flags' dict to each agent's return schema tracking three epistemic humility dimensions from the paper: 'conflict_identified' (bool: detected contradictory data sources), 'conflict_resolved' (bool: agent chose definitive answer vs. acknowledged ambiguity), and 'escalated_to_user' (bool: flagged uncertainty in final output). Modify orchestrator/synthesis.py to aggregate these flags and prepend warnings like 'CONFLICTING SIGNALS DETECTED' to recommendations when conflict_identified=True but conflict_resolved=False, preventing overconfident outputs when fundamental vs. technical analysis disagree or when RSS sentiment contradicts institutional flows.

## Abstract

arXiv:2607.28666v1 Announce Type: new 
Abstract: Enterprise AI programmes stall at a rate that is widely quoted and poorly explained. This paper measures the mechanism. Six document-heavy workflows of the kind performed daily in regulated financial services were run across four model families and three tool configurations, three times each, producing 5,093 scored output elements across 72 configurations. Each configuration was assessed twice: against a demonstration bar, being a single correct run on a single case, and against a production bar requiring sustained accuracy, reproducibility across repeats, verifiable attribution, and a confidence signal that carries information. 57 of 72 configurations cleared the demonstration bar and 32 cleared the production bar, a survival rate of 56.1%.

## Proposed Change

Add a mandatory 'confidence_score' field (0-100) and 'attribution_sources' list to every agent's return schema in agents/base_agent.py. Modify the LangGraph orchestrator in orchestrator/synthesis.py to compute a weighted confidence threshold and flag outputs below the 70th percentile for mandatory human review, logging the review_required boolean to Supabase. This implements the paper's finding that confidence signals reduce review burden from 100% to 49% while maintaining accuracy.

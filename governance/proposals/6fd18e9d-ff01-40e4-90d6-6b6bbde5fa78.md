## Abstract

arXiv:2611.04070v1 Announce Type: new 
Abstract: Researchers ask large language model (LLM) research agents whether a forecasting model beats a static benchmark, a verdict that real data cannot check. We treat the agent as a statistical test and score pre-specified runs of three frontier models and one open-weight model in simulated worlds with a planted signal. The agents detect weak signals more often than a pre-specified out-of-sample test, mainly because they accept weaker evidence: held to an agent's false-positive rate, a conventional in-sample test does as well or better. The agents claim predictability too often in worlds without a signal, state miscalibrated probabilities, and change their verdicts after a sentence about prior research; the magnitudes depend on the model.

## Abstract

Abstract: Reasoning domains have shaped the post-training recipes of frontier models even though they are trained and deployed in domains beyond math and code. In this paper, we study the consequences of directly adopting reasoning-centric training recipes, e.g., the use of outcome-based rewards for reinforcement learning (RL), injection of "cognitive" behaviors such as proposing and backtracking from hypotheses in reasoning chains, etc., for knowledge tasks. We hypothesize that these decisions, while useful for reasoning, inadvertently encourage models to memorize hallucinations they themselves generate in their long reasoning chains when trained on knowledge tasks. ## Proposed Change

In `governance/hallucination_detector.py`, add a function `track_self_reinforced_hallucinations()` that compares Claude Sonnet's synthesised output claims against each agent's raw `detail` dict and `data_sources` list, then logs any claim that (a) appears nowhere in agent outputs and (b) was also present in a prior synthesis for the same stock ticker within the last 30 days. The function should maintain a Supabase table `hallucination_memory` with columns `(ticker, claim_text, claim_embedding vector(1536), first_seen_at, recurrence_count, reinforced)`, using pgvector cosine similarity (threshold 0.92) to detect semantically equivalent repeated hallucinations. Any claim with `recurrence_count >= 2` triggers a `FLAGGED_REINFORCED` label and is injected as a negative-example prefix into the next orchestrator prompt via a `suppression_context` field, explicitly instructing Claude Sonnet not to repeat the flagged assertion. This directly counters the paper's finding that self-generated hallucinations receiving repeated training/inference signal are memorised at ~28x the rate of unreinforced ones.

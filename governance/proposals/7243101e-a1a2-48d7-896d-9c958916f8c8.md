## Abstract

arXiv:2605.00505v1 Announce Type: new 
Abstract: Modern information retrieval (IR) is no longer consumed primarily by humans but increasingly by large language models (LLMs) via retrieval-augmented generation (RAG) and agentic search. Unlike human users, LLMs are constrained by limited attention budgets and are uniquely vulnerable to noise; misleading or irrelevant information is no longer just a nuisance, but a direct cause of hallucinations and reasoning failures. In this perspective paper, we argue that denoising-maximizing usable evidence density and verifiability within a context window-is becoming the primary bottleneck across the full information access pipeline. ## Proposed Change

Add a denoising layer in agents/historical_rag.py before pgvector similarity search that filters chunks by verifiability score. Implement a pre-retrieval scorer (using Haiku API) that assigns each embedded chunk a 0-1 verifiability score based on presence of quantitative data, attribution, and recency, then only retrieve chunks scoring >0.6. This addresses the paper's core thesis that LLMs suffer from noisy context and aligns with the 'unverifiable' stage by prioritizing evidence density.

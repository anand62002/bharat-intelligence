## Abstract

arXiv:2611.03985v1 Announce Type: cross 
Abstract: Large language models (LLMs) read company news at scale, and backtests suggest that their readings predict stock returns. We ask how much of that predictability survives a test that rules out look-ahead, memorization and selection. We propose OLIVIA, a protocol for such tests: point-in-time inputs with company identities masked, selection on validation data only, an analysis plan frozen by cryptographic hash, and a test period unblinded once through a logged lock. We apply it to AI decision models, non-generative language models that return probabilities for typed questions, reading news about S&amp;P 500 stocks: an open-weights model we fine-tuned on 2024-2025 data, three commercial models and standard baselines. ## Proposed Change

Add a 'news_staleness_filter' to the sentiment agent (agents/sentiment.py) that timestamps each RSS feed item, calculates time-since-publication, and downgrades signals for news older than 30 minutes before market open (9:15 AM IST). The paper shows news is priced by open; acting on stale news (overnight headlines) yields zero post-open alpha. Implement by parsing RSS <pubDate>, computing delta vs NSE open time, and multiplying sentiment score by max(0, 1 - staleness_penalty) where penalty = 0.5 if >30min old, 0.8 if >2hrs.

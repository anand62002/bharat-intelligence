## Abstract

arXiv:2610.27027v1 Announce Type: new 
Abstract: Multi-agent LLM systems improve performance on clinical tasks, and that improvement is often read as evidence of a more trustworthy system. However, the two are not the same claim. A clinician acts on the stated reasoning and not only on the conclusion, so whether collaboration reduces hallucination, and what decides when it does, has to be measured in the reasoning too. Yet most evaluations score only the final answer. We evaluate four systems (a single-LLM baseline and three multi-agent topologies) across four models and three clinical tasks, scoring hallucination, under a factuality and faithfulness taxonomy, inside the rationale each system generates and reporting it alongside the task metric.
